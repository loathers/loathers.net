## General Summary
The Sword of S-Words is actually *not* a weapon. It's a familiar!

As a base familiar, it's a fairy -- no special formula for it, so it increases item drops at a rate of `(55*weight)^0.5 + weight - 3`%. It also strengthens you via three enchantments. First, it counts the number of words starting with "S" in your equipment (let's call it X). It then adds that X to your base weapon damage, and adds X/2 to your muscle, mysticality, and moxie. Cute!

It also will attack your opponent, but it does so in a strange way; if combat text contains any S-words, the sword will attack for 5*X damage, where X is the number of S-words in that round of combat.

Finally, it allows you to cast `%fn, kill a lot of these guys` three times a day as a combat skill with the sword equipped. Once cast, the sword stores the monster you targeted with the skill. While your active familiar, the sword will replace the drops of any monster you fight with the drops of the monster you had targeted with the skill, with up to 100 drop tables per day replaced. The targeted monster is reserved over rollover as well, allowing you to get a few more drops out of one of your targeted monsters, if needed.

## Speedrun Applicability

As a fairy, the sword is the most useful class of familiar, and would have a (mild) case to be brought along with you throughout the day. It's unlikely that the Sword of S-Words will be your primary fairy in a run, but it isn't completely out of the picture, as you are almost certainly spending around 100 turns with the sword every day. So long as the Chest Mimic and the Cooler Yeti are in standard, you probably spend more time with those, but maybe not as much as you'd think. The mid-fight attacks and the extremely minor weapon damage and stat boosts are fine, but not particularly powerful.

The "kill a lot of these guys" skill? Now **THAT'S** powerful.

A few years ago, there was a challenge path called Wildfire. Some of you may remember it. The path's main diversion from a normal ascension was that every zone was on fire, which meant every item burned up unless you used different special things to grab the item. Things like the industrial fire extinguisher, or the in-path pickpocket mechanic, or specific resource expenditures to clear zones of fire. In the first few weeks, prior to running the path, there was a lot of talk about how hard the path might end up given how many items you actually needed to get in a run.

Well. By the end of the path, it was clear that "every item burns up except for a handful of specifically targeted drops" actually isn't that big of a deal. It isn't a big deal because it turns out that in any given run, some obscene majority of the monsters you fight don't actually have item drops you care about. Don't get it twisted -- there are a handful of really important things you don't want to miss. But there aren't THAT many really important things you don't want to miss. This means that an item like this that replaces the drop tables of (again) 100 monsters per day is, frankly, *completely bonkers*. It really isn't that hard to find 100 turns where you simply do not care about the drops, so the only item of real strategic import you have to think about is what items you're going to target with your Sword of S-Words. 

## A Bunch of S-Word Targets

Here are a bunch of items you could target with your Sword of S-Words. I've ranked these roughly by quality, with a rough estimate of how many turns are saved by generating this item with your Sword, and a rough estimate of how many you need and how many turns it would take to get that amount if you are rolling with +200% item drop. (Realistically, you will have more in some paths and less in others, but it's a good small-c conservative estimate.)

> **SHADOW BRICKS** (saves 12-13 turns a day)
- Set your S-Word on a SHADOW SLAB
- You can use 13 per day; in a 2-day run, you want to generate 26
- At 200% +item, it takes 43.3 S-Word turns for 13 bricks (...and 86.6 for 26)

Shadow bricks are a pretty obvious one; each brick generated up to 13 gives you a free kill, strictly saving one turn per brick. One freekill in exchange for 3-4 familiar turns is a very easy tradeoff and one of the best things you can do with your S-Word. You still need to summon and kill one shadow slab in order to start it out, so you do lose out on one turn from summoning/fighting the slab (hence the 12-13 turns per day saved rather than 13), but you helpfully can generate all the bricks you'll need for the whole run from that one slab. So that's neat!

> **FREE SMUT ORC BRIDGE** (saves 8-14 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on a SMUT ORC JACKER/PIPELAYER/NAILER/SCREWER
- You need 30 fasteners and 30 planks (or 25, with Bat Wings)
- At 200% +item, it takes 100 turns to get a bridge without swapping targets, or ~50 turns with it

This one is a bit interesting. The orcs in the Smut Orc Logging Camp always drop one fastener and one plank. One is dropped at a 100% rate, the other at a 10% rate. This means at 200% item drop, you need 100 turns to drop 30 of the "10% rate" bridge piece. However, there's an alternate method to get your bridge that takes a lot less time. In this method, spend 25 turns targeting a monster that drops a fastener at a 100% rate, then 25 turns targeting a monster that drops a plank at a 100% rate. In 25 turns, you'd expect 7-8 of the 10% drop, which is enough to give you 30 of both bridge part. 

On the number of turns saved, it is worth noting that there are other ways to get bridge parts. The Sept-Ember Censer gives 5/5 bridge parts once per day, and Bat Wings lowers the # you need a bit as well. If you are getting all your bridge parts from adventuring in the zone, and you own zero of the items that help out with the bridge, you'll need anywhere from 12-16 turns to get all your bridge parts. But each helper does considerably lower the amount of turns you actually need, which thus lowers the full-run turn-saving impact of getting a free bridge. (It's also worth noting that you do need to fight two orcs to do the target-swap version of this usage, which is 2 turns spent. Hence why this saves at max 14 turns.)

> **FREE DEFILED NOOK** (saves 9-11 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on a TOOTHY SKLELTON or a SPINY SKELELTON
- You need 13 Evil Eyes to get to the boss
- At 200% +item, it takes 21.7 turns to get 13 evil eyes

There are a bunch of different ways to tackle the Defiled Nook nowadays. You can get to 400% item drop, banish one monster, and spend 10 turns in the Nook. You can spend refract charges to make your party skeletons worth 2x evil eyes. You can summon skeleton astronauts for dupable eyes and do a bunch of silly nonsense involving your goose and your camel and a bunch of random janky stuff in unrestricted to get 30-something eyes out of two fights. Or, you can ignore all of that and simply use your S-Word to generate a bunch of evil eyes and not have to worry about it at all. As evil eyes are a 20% base drop rate, the rate of return isn't even that bad here; just a few turns is all you really need!

> **INK BLADDERS** (saves ... a lot (?) of turns)
- Set your S-Word on a GIANT SQUID
- You will want quite a few of these
- At 200% +item, you get 1 every 1.1 turns. It's a 30% drop, you probably cap it!

I hesitate to add this one, because it is a relatively recent strategy proposed by Professor Accodorian, and highly likely to be nerfed because it's a little -too- good. But as of this writing (August 3rd, 2026), it still works, and it can't be used in the active challenge path. So it's still worth mentioning! Most free-run items in the game are subject to the restrictions of [Everything Looks Green](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Everything_Looks_Green) (ELG), an effect that prevents you from using another freerun item. In standard, there are very few items left that allow you to run away without spending a turn. Ink bladders are one of them. They are a 30% drop from a monster in the Mariana Trench, the Giant Squid. They're an item that will immediately escape from an underwater combat. 80% of the time, the run is free. 20% of the time, the run takes a turn. 

There is a load-bearing word there: *underwater*. They only work on underwater zones. However, due to the ever-hilarious creeping value of the <a href="/analysis/2025-09-monodent">Monodent of the Sea</a>, you can currently turn one zone per day into an underwater zone via the "summon a wave" skill. What this means is that you stand to benefit greatly if you generate a patently absurd number of ink bladders via one Chest Mimic summon of a giant squid. You can do something like a full-burn of D1 delay in the Penultimate Fantasy Airship and a full-burn of D2 delay in Copperhead Club. Doing that would take anywhere from 35-45 ink bladders, depending on how you're solving the airship's necessary items (i.e., the mohawk wig and the amulet) and whether you want to use banishes on a monster or two in the club for easy access to specific heartstone letters.

I do want to explicitly note: this is almost certainly going to be nerfed very soon. The most likely nerf is that ink bladders impart some amount of the effect Everything Looks Green, like other freeruns. However, given what TPTB did with T.U.R.D.S. keys and Short Writ of Habeas Corpus (both of which are a limited-use free run item that got to have a lower ELG cooldown due to their limited use conventions), there is a pretty decent chance that the final ELG value they settle on for Ink Bladders will keep this as a useful target for the S-Word, given the relatively high 30% item drop rate and the fun synergy. It also has the benefit of involving an evergreen perma-standard item, which means that it is a relatively resilient strategy once the ELG value is decided. 

> **EVERYTHING LOOKS GREEN FREERUNS** (saves 2-3 turns... or a lot more!)
- Set your S-Word on a PYGMY WITCH LAWYER or GREEN OPS SOLDIER
- You will want 13+ GSB/Writs
- At 200% +item, it takes 86.7 turns to get 13 GSB/Writs 

On the subject of free runs that use [Everything Looks Green](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Everything_Looks_Green) (ELG), the aforementioned Short Writs and Green Smoke Bombs are two other possible S-Word targets. GSBs are universally usable, with 25 turns of ELG. Writs are only beneficial on Pygmies, with 20 turns of ELG. Both of these are plausible targets for your S-Word once the <a href="/analysis/2024-07-roman-candelabra">Roman Candelabra</a> and <a href="/analysis/2024-02-spring-shoes">Spring Shoes</a> cycle out of Standard. They're a somewhat annoyign target, though. Both are 5% drops, which are awful to generate -- it basically will take up an entire day of your S-Word's turns. 

In the event you have access to a free 30-turn ELG freerun, these aren't particularly helpful as a S-Word target. In a 2-day 300 turn run, you get 11 useful 30-turn free runs. If you were to swap all those to 25-turn freeruns, you'd get 13 -- so two turns saved for about 90 S-Word turns spent. Writs are nice, but you generally cannot open the Hidden City for 50-ish turns anyway, and you lack the ability to use them the entire run, as you need to conclude the Hidden City before doing 30-40 turns of nonsense in the Pyramid. So you also lack the ability to just do a 1-to-1 swap of all 30-turn runs for 20-turn runs. Still, it's probably worth 3-4 runs over the course of your run if you do all runs from (say) turn 40 to turn 220 with short writs rather than your candles or your shoes.

It is also worth noting, again, that the equation changes once those two leave standard. Without a source for ELG runs, these are pure profit, saving you 13+ turns a run. Generating a ton of green smoke bombs doesn't sound like a particularly -fun- way to use your S-Word turns, but it certainly is a reasonable one. (Especially if TPTB ever nerf the out-of-standard combat item exploit that makes Shadow Bricks such an obviously dominant option, since that'll open up a good 40-80 turns for use with this green ops soldier nonsense.)

> **TOMB RATCHETS** (saves 4-5 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on a TOMB RAT
- You need 10 tomb ratchets
- At 200% +item, it takes 17 turns to get 10 tomb ratchets

Realistically, you probably don't -actually- need 10 tomb ratchets. Assuming you aren't doing some wacky unrestricted jank, you will always get 1 wheel from the upper chamber's semi-guaranteed noncombat, which is interchangable with a ratchet. You also will probably get a few while you are adventuring in the middle chamber as well. It's a very good zone to use some free-run banishes, and if you're down to just tomb rats, you're pretty likely to get a ratchet or two in the 8 turns of combat-delay you've got in the zone. Still, this is a plausibly turn-saving usage of the S-Word for two reasons. 

First, it's pretty common to have a few ratchets remaining despite your best efforts, and this saves any extra turns in the middle chamber spent getting those extra ratchets. Second, you can use this as an excuse to -really- pump noncombat rate in the tavern without having to focus on getting a tangle of rat tails. While tangles are generally considered semi-essential items (as they save extra turns spent in the middle chamber), every tangle does technically cost a turn when compared to the skippable noncombats in the typical tavern. You may be able to pump a bit more -com in exchange for some of your ML items in the tavern, snagging an extra turn or two. Just a thought!

> **A-BOO CLUES** (saves 1-3 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on ANY GHOST AT A-BOO PEAK
- You need 4 A-Boo Clues
- At 200% +item, it takes 8.9 turns to get 4 A-Boo Clues

We are entering the realm of "technically useful, realistically mid-to-bad" targets. A-Boo Clues are quite useful; you're going to want to get them every run. But there are two easier ways to get the clues you need. The first is refracting a summoned "some fish" and yellow-raying it through the synergy between the <a href="/analysis/2025-09-monodent">Monodent of the Sea</a> and the <a href="/analysis/2025-10-blood-cubic-zirconia/">Blood Cubic Zirconia</a>. (And a yellow ray source, too.) It's wonky, but it is a very nice way to solve the A-Boo Peak problem. The second evergreen source is to just spend two clovers there. Two clovers and four clue turns finishes your peak, and is (probably) preferable to the S-Word, which technically only saves you one turn and two clovers over that method. If you are low on shinies and end up tight on clovers, this is a decent usage. But I'm not really sure it's ever going to be more than a very marginal option for optimizing your S-Word.

> **RUSTY HEDGE TRIMMERS** (saves 1-3 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on ANY TWIN PEAK TOPIARY ANIMAL
- You need 0-4 Rusty Hedge Trimmers
- At 200% +item, it takes 8.9 turns to get 4 rusty hedge trimmers

Similar to A-Boo clues, this isn't necessarily a big piece of the puzzle, but it's a nice end-of-run thing you can pull on if nothing else is left. The thing with Twin Peak is that the zone has a 90% combat rate. Since we can pretty trivially get to -25% combat, that means that every turn you spend in twin peak has a 35% (or more) chance of just generating the noncombat you need anyway. So, 1/3 turns you spend in the zone will probably be a noncombat. Given that fact, you don't necessarily need that many hedge trimmers in the first place. And given that you need to fight one topiary animal in order to set the S-Word, you might as well just (as with A-Boo clues) use a monodent/BCZ/yellow ray combo to generate 3 hedge trimmers in one fight while also keeping NC rate on so that you have a chance of just getting the NC anyway. Still, it's not a bad possible last target, and is better if you're missing some of the aforementioned items.

> **BARRELS OF GUNPOWDER** (saves 4-30 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on a LOBSTERFROGMAN
- You need 5 barrels of gunpowder
- At any +item at all, it takes 5 turns to get 5 barrels of gunpowder 

I don't think this will actually be a very good usage for someone with a full standard set at any point in this IOTM's standard lifecycle, as the superior solution to the lobsterfrogman problem is to eat some <a href="/analysis/2026-05-legendary-pasta-wand">Legendary Pasta Wand</a> food to guarantee combats and spend 5 turns at Sonofa Beach. But if you are lacking the pasta wand, this is actually a pretty great use of the sword. And it's certainly possible we get a path or two where you can't eat food, at which point this may come up again as a good use. Even with +20% combat rate, Sonofa Beach still takes anywhere from 25-35 turns without other ways to guarantee an LFM, so swapping that out with one summon and 4 familiar turns is an easy win for the intrepid adventurer. Again, I don't really think it's worth it otherwise (as it's only a 4-turn save), but it's a tool to have in your toolbox.

> **SONARS-IN-A-BISCUIT** (saves 1-4 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on ANY BAT THAT DROPS A SONAR 
- You need 3 sonars-in-a-biscuit 
- At +200% +item, it takes 10 turns to get 3 sonars (or 7 turns, if you copy a ratbat)

So long as this shares a standard set with the <a href="/analysis/2024-10-bat-wings">Bat Wings</a>, this isn't really useful at all; you get 2 of the 3 sonars you need for free. But it may actually warrant a short look later. You don't really want to spend turns in any of the bat zones, and it's sort of a crappy refract target because you need to waste a yellow ray on it. The idea of summoning up an enchanted beanbat and using the S-Word to ensure you open up all your zones may actually beat generating them naturally, especially given how the <a href="/analysis/2026-03-Archaeologist's-Spade">Archaeologist's Spade</a> has made the ratbat burrow an inferior Shen snake zone as compared to the cemetery. (One exceedingly minor note: if you are doing this for some reason, there's a (very minor) case for summoning a ratbat so that you can passively generate some [Ratbatatouille](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Ratbatatouille) in order to generate your legendary noodle food. I don't really think it's that big of a deal, but it's something you can theoretically do.)

> **BOWLING BALLS** (saves 1-3 turns in a run)
- Set your S-Word on a PYGMY BOWLER 
- You need 5 bowling balls (but you'll get 1 with the bowler, so really 4)
- At >150% +item, it takes 4 turns to generate 4 bowling balls 

I don't really like this one at all, but I'm mentioning it because it is begrudgingly *could* be worth the same 3-4 turn amount that the above few are worth. My thing with bowling balls is that there are a lot of different (better) ways to generate a lot of bowling balls from a paucity of combats. It's an easy no-yellow-ray-needed refract target, it's a great shrunken head target, a solid "club across the battlefield" target, and it's a very good pickpocket target. You also can get them from the noncombat in the Hidden Park. Still, this does (technically) save a few turns, so I'm including it.

> **TURN GENERATION**

I'm not going to include the projection bit, because there are a lot of different options for turn generation. My favorite is the **CRAGGY BARTENDER**, which has three 15% drops. All are 3-potency 12-turngen booze, and they're TTT drops, so they're evergreen. At 4 adv/drunk, it's not exactly changing your life, but it's mildly helpful if you want to boost the value of your liver, so perhaps +5-10 turns versus the alternatives. If you have access to pastamastery and pulverize, using this on a **FLEET WOODSMAN** to get a bunch of fleetwood chains is a good way to boost your adventure output from your stomach, as that should allow you to get enough fleetwood mac 'n' cheeses to fill 12 of your fullness per day with 5 adv/fullness foods. This is probably worth a few more turns, something like +10-15 turns per day. A potentially good option if you're nearing a daycount barrier!

## Planning Out Your S-Words

I will admit -- usage of the S-Word is a little more annoying to plan than some of our other IOTMs. Because you are just rolling for a monster's item at your own active drop rate, you have to generate estimates of how many turns it will (probably) take to get the number of items you want. I used +200% item drop as the baseline for the above estimates, but any speed ascender knows that your character's item drop rate flits around during a run depending on what equipment you can reasonably equip, buffs you happen to have on, and the weight of your item-drop familiar (in this case, your S-Word). ## 2026 In-Standard Synergies

- There's high S-Word synergy with the <a href="analysis/2025-05-peridot/">Peridot of Peril</a> (2025) and the <a href="/analysis/2024-01-chest-mimic/">Chest Mimic</a> (2024). When you want to switch targets, you want to encounter whatever you're switching to as quickly as possible, and being able to generate the target monster at-will via the Peridot or the Mimic is great. (Obviously, the mimic's value is particularly glaring for things like shadow slabs, but it's still true for other uses too.)
- As noted throughout the article, there are areas of overlap where the S-Word provides alternate solutions to things we would traditionally use other IOTMs for. (As an example, there's little need to use the Baseball Diamond for shadow bricks anymore, given the alternate acquisition method represented by the S-Word.)
- This is yet another IOTM that pairs nicely with the <a href="/analysis/2025-09-monodent">Monodent of the Sea</a> (2025). Because you're using the S-Word to replace the monster's drop table, the actual monster you fight doesn't matter all that much, which allows you to use the scaling Some Fish monster instead of whatever crappy monster you'd be fighting otherwise in many zones. This is helpful for statgain, which doesn't matter much in the Censer's standard set, but will matter a bit more in the 2027 and 2028 standard sets. 
- Two important anti-synergies worth noting -- free kills that destroy a monster's drop table also destroy the drop table of the monster your S-Word is targeting. This means you can't get items out of your lianas, or drunk pygmies, or on fights where you assert your authority, or (out of standard) fights where you use groveling gravel. You'll just waste one of your 100 S-Word turns with no chance of gaining the associated monster's drop. This also applies to <a href="/analysis/2025-10-blood-cubic-zirconia/">BCZ</a> (2025) refracts; the way the S-Word works is that it completely destroys the drop table of the monster you are fighting. Refraction modifies the monster's drop table, but that modification is overridden by the S-Word. So... don't refract-and-sword!
- This is more of an unrestricted synergy than a standard synergy, but is worth noting. In unrestricted, there are a ton of "low quality" free fights -- fights where you really don't care about the monster's base drop table. The S-Word synergizes *really well* with these, as you can do these fights at times where you've got excessively high item drop and finish up your S-Word drops quicker than you would during lay adventuring. In standard, there aren't quite as many of these as you have in unrestricted, but a small list of useful examples might include: skeleton fights via the <a href="/analysis/2026-03-Archaeologist's-Spade">Archaeologist's Spade</a> (2026), infernal seal fights for seal clubbers, science tent tentacles, and <a href="/analysis/2025-12-cyberrealm">CyberRealm</a> (2025) fights. 

## Overall Rating

We'd rate the Sword of S-Words a tier 0 IOTM. That's right -- top tier! In the Ascension Speed Society Discord, there was some talk about this IOTM being mid or bad. I don't really get it! Generating your bricks via familiar turns is amazing; marginal familiar turns are worth extremely little in standard, with 25-30 turns exchanging into one turn saved with a handful of split pea soup. This is an awesome IOTM that allows you to save 12-13 turns per day with your shadow bricks, 9-10 turns per run with your evil eyes, 8-14 turns per run with your bridge, and an assortment of 3-6 turnsave uses for your other few uses. I have trouble imagining a world where we aren't using 100 turns of this guy every single day. Put it all together and you're easily saving 20 or so turns per day. This is an absolutely bonkers IOTM. Perhaps one of the best to ever do it!

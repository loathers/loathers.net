For August's IOTM, the powers that be ended up taking inspiration from the wonders of numismatics. If you have never heard of that word before, well. Congratulations! You've led a great life. It's the history of currency, and it's the most boring thing you'll ever hear about. The only reason I know about it is that during the COVID work-from-home period, my employer used to have us all do show-and-tell during our weekly team meetings. One of my old coworkers chose to use his slot to talk about numismatics for 30 minutes. I'll never forget it -- these were perhaps the worst 15 minutes of my life. If anyone ever wants to tell you about numismatics, you should ask them to just give you some money instead and let you study the currency from home. And then you should make a wise financial decision and put that currency in a high-interest savings account to celebrate the oddity that is the August 2026 IOTM: the **Interesting Coin**.

## General Summary

The interesting coin is a combat item. It is also a currency, and also quest item. Upon ascending into a new run, you are sent four interesting coins. You gain more coins each day; if you have zero coins, you gain 1 coin at rollover. If you have anywhere from 1 to 20 coins, you will get 3 coins each rollover. After that, you will gain 20% interest on your coins, rounded down to the nearest whole coin. (So, for instance: at 22 coins, you gain 4. At 100 coins, you gain 20. At 500 coins, you gain 100. And so on and so forth.)

As a combat item, you can use the coin once per day to free-kill a monster. You can do this once per day; happily, this use does not actually use up the coin. So you are free to spend that coin, if you so desire.

As a currency, you can spend your coins on a large number of different options. Realistically, for the purposes of speed-running, there are three items you'll be spending your coins on in-run.

- First, there's the **LIQUID ASSET** (1 🪙). This is a 1-toxicity spleen item that gives you one cast of Exercise Liquidity. This skill will replace your current monster with a different monster in the current zone, similar to [macrometeorite](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Macrometeorite), [CHEAT CODE: Replace Enemy](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/CHEAT_CODE:_Replace_Enemy), or <a href="/analysis/2023-08-scepter">waffles</a>.
- Second, there's the **INTANGIBLE ASSET** (1 🪙). This is a 1-toxicity spleen item. When consumed, the meat you gain from your next resolved combat will be translated into stats -- 50% to your mainstat, 25% to each offstat. (The meat gained is responsive to increased meat drop percent.)
- Finally, there's the **TOXIC ASSET** (1 🪙). This is a 1-toxicity spleen item that forces the next monster you encounter to drop all its items. This does *not* add any turns of Everything Looks Yellow; it's akin to a [Spooky Jelly](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Spooky_jelly), essentially.

There are also a handful of other small items that are technically affordable in a 2-day run, although they are extremely unlikely to be purchased by most speedrunners.

- The **invisible hand** (2 🪙) gives 30 turns of +250% muscle.
- The **circle of overdraft protection scroll** (2 🪙) gives 30 turns of +250% myst.
- The **mint** (2 🪙) gives 30 turns of +250% moxie and +50% init.
- The **homeowner's loam** (3 🪙) grows your garden with one day's growth. (Currently useless in standard; no standard gardens!)
- The **savings bondo** (4 🪙) gives 20 turns of "Saving Some Bondo", which translates to 4 free crafts (as 5 turns of the effect are consumed with each turn-taking craft you embark on with the effect active).

You also can (technically) purchase the solvent (removes all negative effects, 3 🪙), Roth IPA (a 5 adv/drunk size-1 booze, 4 🪙), soybean futures (1000 meat in a weird way, 7 🪙), and underdraft protection (+20 sleaze damage pants, 7 🪙). Please don't, though.

## Speedrun Applicability, but fake (aka non-spleeners)

As usual, let's handle the easy stuff first. The singular free-kill per day is nice. It's not a super high quality free-kill, given the item/meat destruction, but it's a blisteringly simple way to save a single turn each day.

Unlike many IOTMs, you do have a decent amount of choice with how you spend your currency. There are some pretty obvious uses that will likely be the main uses for speedsters in the next few standard sets, but there's enough flexibility that one or two path boards will probably be swung by a creative application of the lesser choices. To wit, here are a few possible uses of the road-less-traveled options in the coin shop.

- Using the +250% stat items to help with leveling or tower tests on D2 could be legitimately useful in some avatar path.
- The loam allowing for +1 day of garden growth is quite interesting; it's actually quite good in unrestricted, where that may translate to 5-6 turns off the War battlefield via extra stuffing fluffers. It is totally useless in standard as of now, but could rise in the future if a new IOTM garden is released.
- The savings bondo allowing you to have 4 free crafts is worth at least one turn per run if you have no other free craft options, as speedsters will always be cooking a Wine Bomb. It also has minor synergy with all the smithing you'd like to do for your <a href="/analysis/2026-07-cup-of-13s">Cup of 13s</a> (2026).

## Speedrun Applicability, but for real (aka The Spleeners)

... OK, done with the easy stuff. The spleeners are where the coin's power really lies, and by far the most useful piece of this IOTM. The worst of the spleeners (**toxic asset**, the spooky jelly lookalike) is still pretty dang good; 1 spleen for a forced drop that doesn't impact your Everything Looks Yellow timer is pretty good. In the event that TPTB do not reprint an ELY-powered yellow ray source, this will quite easily take the place as the 2nd best spleener; the first 3-4 yellow rays in a run are extremely valuable and important, especially in the age of refraction. 

While we still have ELY yellow rays, though, the **liquid asset** is the true 2nd best spleener. And you know what, it's pretty great. Monster replacers are an interesting tool. In theory, they are worse than a free-run banish; if you don't want the monster you have encountered, it's not *necessarily* a given that you want any old random other monster in the zone, especially since the replacer doesn't actually burn delay. I actually wrote a bit about monster replacers in the long-ago analysis of the <a href="/analysis/2023-08-scepter/">August Scepter's waffles</a>; that commentary still holds for the liquid asset. To add a bit more modern IOTM color, replacers synergize well with a lot of different IOTM-based combat actions. Three specific ones of note: 

- The swoop from <a href="/analysis/2024-10-bat-wings">Bat Wings</a> (2024) allows you to snag an item from the monster you originally encountered. This is super useful for things like bowlers, where you can swoop a bowling ball, change your monster to something else via cleesh or summon some fish, and then use the liquid asset to roll for another bowler without spending a turn.
- Because we have a mid-combat banisher in the Spring Kick from <a href="/analysis/2024-02-spring-shoes/">the Spring Shoes</a> (2024), you can also use replacers to essentially get a free shot at another monster in the zone. Say you encounter a slick lihc in the Defiled Niche; you can spring kick that slick lihc, then use liquid asset to hop into either a Dirty Old Lihc (who you can sniff) or another bad lihc (who you can banish with another source). Two banishes for the price of one!
- The counter for damage from the <a href="/analysis/2025-11-shrunken-head">shrunken head</a> (2025) resets when you replace your monster with another, which lets you stack more damage on your zombie to kill it a bit quicker.

One other fun fact -- the way that the Boss Bat works is different than virtually every other boss in the game. Instead of just forcing himself into the picture as a superlikely adventure, the Boss Bat is added as a mob in the zone somewhere from turns 4-6 in the zone. You aren't allowed to banish the guards, but you -can- use replacers in the zone. This is -not- as good as a waffle; waffles will just refuse your replace if there aren't any other monsters in the zone. But using one at 5 turns spent in the Boss Bat's Chamber is a decent use of a late-run liquid asset, if you roll a bodyguard on turn 5.

Finally, let's cover the actual best-in-class spleen item from this IOTM: the **INTANGIBLE ASSET**. 

Leveling in KOL is often somewhat hard, and often somewhat annoying. As the speedrunning community re-learned for the Thrifty challenge path, there are a lot of substats you need to get all the way to the sorceress tower. There are major routing benefits to getting these stats as early as humanly possible, allowing you to combine quests that need similar resources and use one resource for multiple tasks. The last four "eras" of KOL standard sets have had their own specific solutions for leveling. I have invented names for them, because I want to.

- 2024-2026 was/is the **"CENSER-SHIP" era**. We relied on the <a href="/analysis/2024-09-sept-ember-censer">Sept-Ember Censer</a>, which generates substats via potions that scale based on cold resistance. 
- 2023-2024 was the **"CHOO CHOO" era**. We relied on the [Model Train Set](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Model_train_set), a workshed item that generated massive amounts of stats in accordance with the stations you'd set up for the train to cycle through as you ran combats. (Some paths also relied on the Cincho de Mayo's substat-doubler skill, though those were somewhat rare.)
- 2021-2022 was the **"KITCHEN SINK" era**. In-between the tote and the trainset, we relied on a pretty wacky array of different items; the [emotion chip](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Emotionally_Chipped)'s "Feel Pride" skill allowed you to triple the statgain from 3 fights a day, the [unbreakable umbrella](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Unbreakable_umbrella_(broken)) allowed you to add +25% ML to fights, and the [Grey Goose](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Grey_Goose) had a valuable skill that allowed you to gain 3375 substats for 375 familiar XP. Also, we had the [red rocket](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/Ready_to_Eat) from the clan fireworks shop, which gave you 5x statgains from the next thing you ate every 100 turns, which was a nice little booster.
- 2018-2020 was the **"WEARING GARBAGE"** era. We relied heavily on [January's Garbage Tote](https://wiki.kingdomofloathing.com/January%27s_Garbage_Tote), whose makeshift garbage tote allowed you to double the statgains from 37 fights per day. (This synergized nicely with sources of free scaling fights, like the Pocket Professor, Kramco, and the neverending party.)

With this IOTM, I can officially welcome our readers to **the EMBEZZLEMENT era**.

Each time you consume an intangible asset, your next fight will convert all dropped meat into substat gain -- 50% to your mainstat, 25% to your offstats. For most fights, this isn't a particularly useful or interesting boon. But for certain monsters, this actually represents a bonkers amount of statgain. One particularly obvious target for this is the Knob Goblin Embezzler, the Lucky! adventure in Cobb's Knob Treasury. The KGE has 1000 base meat drop; this means that if you have 0% added meat drop and 0% additional mainstat per fight, you'd get 500 mainstat per fight. But we can do a hell of a lot better than that. Using only IOTMs that will be available in next year's standard...

- Evergreen skills immediately give us +130% -- +50% from polka of plenty, +20% from nimble fingers, and +10% from gnomish pickpocketing, thief among the honorable, expert panhandling, thrift & grift, your Lasagnmbie, and disco leer. 
- For essentially no cost, you can get +40% from BCZ's sweat equity, +30% from the <a href="/analysis/2025-04-april-shower-thoughts/">April Shower calendar</a>, and +30% from the <a href="/analysis/2025-03-leprecondo">Leprecondo</a> (overall, +100%)
- For some liver space, you get +100% from the <a href="/analysis/2026-07-cup-of-13s">Cup of 13s</a> booze buff, and +100% from the Prize Turkey from the <a href="/analysis/2025-12-skeleton-of-crimbo-past">Skeleton of Crimbo Past</a> (overall, +200%)
- You get +20% meat from equipping your <a href="/analysis/2025-10-blood-cubic-zirconia/">Blood Cubic Zirconia</a> outside of your <a href="/analysis/2026-01-eternity-codpiece">Eternity Codpiece</a>, and +5% from equipping a saucepan.

Add it all up, and this gets you an easy 455% pre-familiar-bonus. Using an evergreen Hobo Monkey at a buffed familiar weight of 35 pounds, that adds another 150% on top. This gets you to a grand total of 605%, but there's a few other sources you may consider. For some hassle, you can get +100% from the <a href="/analysis/2025-07-cooler-yeti">Cooler Yeti</a> buff, +100% from spelling "VIEW" with your <a href="/analysis/2026-02-heartstone">Heartstone</a>, and 40-50% from finding a meaty Shrunken Head zombie; add those in, and you're suddenly at 855% meat drop without using a single rare <a href="/analysis/2025-06-beret">Prismatic Beret</a> buff. 

One fight with an embezzler at 605% meat drop will give you an average of 3000 mainstat per combat, and 855% gets you up to 4250 mainstat. But we can do better than that, too! The stats you gain from intangible asset fights are impacted by any percentage boosts to mainstat per combat. So, atop that, we can add +10% mainstat substats from your moonsign and +25% mainstat substats from the <a href="/analysis/2026-07-portable-laughing-stock/">Laughing Stock</a>. That translates the previous totals from 3000-4250 to 4050-5700. 

Suffice to say: *that's sick!* Level 11 requires 10,816 total substats, and level 12 requires 15,625. Even at this very low end, three turns of embezzler leveling gets you about halfway to level 12, and the high end gets you to level 12 and a bit of the way to 13. This ignores all IOTMs from 2024, too; next year's standard set will be able to achieve this and more, depending on other meat drop items that are added to the standard set as time goes on. 

Now, there's a cost to this. Unlike the Censer, you are spending turns to do this leveling -- 2-3 turns, to be exact. This also costs 2-3 clovers! But a precious few turns and some clovers to solve essentially all of your leveling needs is a trade-off that makes sense essentially every time. You best start believing in embezzler stories, sonnie: *you're in one*.

## Overall Rating

We'd rate the Interesting Coin a tier 1 IOTM. Eagle-eyed readers might recall that I rated the Sept-Ember Censer tier 1 as well. It's a very good IOTM. The weird thing about leveling IOTMs is that the overarching turn savings you get from them are extremely daycount-driven. You save a ton of turns on D1; being able to level without any real effort is worth 10-20 turns over a full run, on the low end for full shiny folks and the high end for low shiny folks. But there are severe diminishing returns; after all, you only need to level to 13, and anything beyond that is questionably useful. Still -- the drop forcer and the monster replacer are both quite valuable little tools for your D2, which helps hold up the value a bit. I still think this is short of the lofty heights you get from a <a href="/analysis/2024-09-sept-ember-censer">Sword of S-Words</a> or a <a href="/analysis/2025-10-blood-cubic-zirconia/">Blood Cubic Zirconia</a>, but it's a critically important IOTM for the next two years, and I **highly** recommend grabbing one.
